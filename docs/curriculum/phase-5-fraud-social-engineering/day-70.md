# Day 70: Recognizing grooming and trafficking indicators from published sources

Phase: 5. Fraud, scam, and social-engineering investigation · Track goal: Know the behavioral indicators that child-protection and anti-trafficking bodies publish, know what those indicators can and cannot tell you, and know exactly who to report to and what never to do while doing it.

## Concept
This day is about recognition and routing. It covers the indicators that bodies like the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), the Canadian Centre for Child Protection (C3P), the U.S. Department of Homeland Security's Blue Campaign, and Polaris publish for parents, educators, platforms, and the public. It does not cover investigating suspected offenders, engaging with them, posing as a child or a buyer, or handling sexual content involving minors. Those are law enforcement functions that need legal authority, specialist training, and welfare support for the investigator. No amount of good intent makes them a solo project, and this curriculum includes no example grooming messages or conversations for the same reason.

Two rules apply before anything else.

First, if you ever encounter what may be sexual imagery of a child, do not download it, screenshot it, save it, print it, or forward it, including to a colleague or to "preserve evidence". In most jurisdictions possessing or distributing it is a crime regardless of intent, and every copy adds to the harm. Write down where you saw it (the URL, platform, and username), leave, and report it (see the routing card below). The Florida Department of Law Enforcement's published guidance says the same.

Second, if a child or any person may be in immediate danger, call emergency services first. Every other step comes after that.

Third, this is recognition and routing, not amateur investigation, and that includes so-called "predator hunting" or "sting" groups that confront suspects themselves, sometimes on camera. Do not join or coordinate with one. They routinely compromise the evidence and the eventual prosecution, expose the person doing the confronting to real legal and physical risk, and DHS's own caution above (never confront a suspected trafficker or signal your suspicions) applies just as much to grooming cases. If you want to act beyond reporting, the lawful path is training and vetting through an actual law-enforcement or NCMEC-affiliated program, not a self-organized group.

### What the published indicators say
NCMEC's online enticement page lists tactics its CyberTipline sees most often, including building rapport through compliments and shared interests, pretending to be younger, offering incentives such as gift cards, and moving toward sexual conversation or requests for images. C3P's Cybertip.ca warns young people about "love-bombing" (a flood of compliments from someone new), fake compatibility (a stranger claiming to share everything), and claims to be the same age, from the same area, or to have mutual friends. NCMEC reports that online enticement reports to the CyberTipline rose more than 300% from 2021 to 2023, and it published formal indicator guidelines for online platforms in October 2024 under the REPORT Act.

Several of these overlap with the romance-scam list from Day 69, so the recognition skills carry over. The difference is who is at risk and who must act: when a minor may be involved, the job is to report quickly to the right body and not to gather more.

For human trafficking, DHS Blue Campaign publishes indicators such as a person who seems disconnected from family and community, appears coached on what to say, is always accompanied by someone who seems in control, has no personal possessions, shows signs of being denied food, sleep, or medical care, or cannot move freely. DHS adds two cautions: no single indicator proves trafficking, and you should never confront a suspected trafficker or signal your suspicions to a possible victim. Polaris goes further in "Know the story, not the signs", arguing that surface signs without context lead to wrong and often biased conclusions, and that the people best placed to help are those with real, ongoing contact (a nurse, a teacher, a regular customer).

The scam-center problem from Day 67 is trafficking as well: people recruited by fake job ads and forced to run fraud. The recruitment red flags you already scored are trafficking indicators when the job involves relocation abroad.

### What an indicator cannot tell you
An indicator raises a question for the people with authority and context to answer it. It does not identify a victim or an offender. A lab that ends with "this person is a trafficker" or "this child is being groomed" has gone past what indicators support. When this taxonomy feeds a screening system (Day 73), a match should route a case to trained human review, never produce an automated accusation.

If you work in a role with a legal mandatory-reporting duty (teaching, healthcare, social work, and others, depending on jurisdiction), that duty is separate from and does not wait on anything in this lab: report through your professional obligation on its own timeline, in addition to whatever this exercise has you build. This day teaches general recognition and routing; it is not a substitute for knowing your own profession's reporting rules.

## Resources
- [NCMEC: Online enticement](https://www.missingkids.org/theissues/onlineenticement): tactics, risk factors, statistics, and the CyberTipline.
- [NCMEC: Guidelines on identifiers of online enticement and child sex trafficking (PDF, 2024)](https://www.missingkids.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/NCMEC-REPORT-Act-Guidelines.pdf): the platform-facing guidelines issued under the REPORT Act. Read the introduction and category structure; you do not need the detail to do this lab.
- [Cybertip.ca online harms](https://cybertip.ca/en/online-harms/) and [alerts](https://cybertip.ca/en/online-harms/alerts/): C3P's public alerts, including sextortion and "image removal" recovery scams.
- [DHS Blue Campaign: How to identify and report human trafficking](https://www.dhs.gov/blue-campaign/identify-victim): indicator list, cautions, and tip lines.
- [DHS Know2Protect: How to report](https://www.dhs.gov/know2protect/how-to-report): U.S. reporting routes for online child exploitation.
- [Polaris: Know the story, not the signs](https://polarisproject.org/know-the-story-not-the-signs/) and [Recognizing labor trafficking](https://polarisproject.org/labor-trafficking/).
- [FDLE: Child sexual abuse material](https://www.fdle.state.fl.us/fco/child-pornography): the do-not-download, do-not-forward guidance.
- [NCMEC Take It Down](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/): helps minors (and adults for images taken when they were minors) get explicit images removed from participating platforms without sending the image itself.

## Practical: draw.io, an indicator source matrix and a reporting routing card

You build two things today. Neither involves collecting data about any real person or any real conversation.

### Part 1: indicator source matrix
In a spreadsheet, build a matrix with these columns: indicator (paraphrased at the category level, as the source states it), category (online enticement, sextortion, sex trafficking, labor trafficking), published by, audience the source wrote it for (parents, youth, platforms, public, law enforcement), what the indicator alone cannot tell you, and the source link.

Fill at least 15 rows from at least four of the sources above. Keep each indicator as general as the source keeps it. If you find yourself adding detail the source did not give, stop and cut it back.

Example rows:

| Indicator | Category | Published by | Audience | Cannot tell you alone | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adult pretends to be younger when contacting a child | Online enticement | NCMEC | Parents, public | Whether the contact is an offender or someone lying for another reason; needs report and review | missingkids.org/theissues/onlineenticement |
| Person appears coached on what to say | Trafficking | DHS Blue Campaign | Public | Whether this is coercion, anxiety, a language barrier, or immigration fear | dhs.gov/blue-campaign/identify-victim |
| Recruiter offers an overseas job with travel and visa "handled" and a vague role | Labor trafficking | OHCHR (2023 scam-centre report) | Policy, public | Whether the offer is fraud, trafficking, or a poorly run legitimate agency | OHCHR report, Day 67 resources |

### Part 2: reporting routing card
In draw.io, build a one-page decision tree for your own jurisdiction that starts with "What did I see?" and ends at a named reporting body with its URL or phone number. It must include these branches:

- Immediate danger to anyone: emergency services first (911 in the U.S. and Canada, 999 in the UK).
- Possible sexual imagery of a child: a "do not download, screenshot, or forward" node, then the reporting body.
- Suspected online enticement or sextortion of a child.
- Suspected human trafficking (sex or labor).
- A scam that may involve trafficked workers (Day 67), which goes to both the fraud reporting route (Day 76) and a trafficking hotline.
- A "do not confront, do not investigate further, do not alert the person" node on the trafficking branch.

Reporting bodies to place on the card, depending on jurisdiction:

| Jurisdiction | Child exploitation | Human trafficking |
|---|---|---|
| United States | NCMEC CyberTipline, report.cybertip.org or 1-800-843-5678 | National Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733; ICE HSI tip line, 1-866-347-2423 |
| Canada | Cybertip.ca | Canadian Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-833-900-1010 |
| United Kingdom | CEOP Safety Centre (ceop.police.uk) for children at risk; Internet Watch Foundation for images | Modern Slavery & Exploitation Helpline, 08000 121 700 |

If you live elsewhere, find your national equivalents on a government or child-protection body's own site, and note on the card where you found each one.

### The artifact
The indicator source matrix (at least 15 rows, four or more sources, every row linked) and the draw.io routing card exported as PDF or PNG.

## Checkpoint
Pick any row in your matrix and show the sentence in the source it came from; if you cannot, delete the row. Your routing card must have the "do not download or forward" node and the emergency-services branch, and every phone number on it must come from the body's own website. Then answer in two or three sentences: why does this curriculum stop at recognition and routing for this topic when other days go on to investigate? A complete answer covers the legal risk of handling imagery and what happens to a victim if an untrained investigator alerts the offender.

If any part of this day was hard to read or brought up something personal, that is a normal reaction to this material. Take a break before Day 71. If you want to talk to someone: in the US, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline); in the UK, Samaritans is 116 123; find your own country's equivalent if you're elsewhere, the same way you did for the routing card above.
